Multicolor Triangle Ramsey Numbers

Color each edge between N points with one of k colors. The Ramsey number Rk(3) is the smallest N that forces a triangle with all three edges in one color. For example, R2(3)=6: among six people, some three are all friends or all strangers.

Exact values are rare. R3(3)=17, and R4(3) is only known to lie between 51 and 62. For large k, the best upper limit was about e⋅k!, and the best lower limit was about 3.28k.

So the open question was whether Rk(3) grows like ck or faster. Erdős offered $100 to decide if the limit of Rk(3)1/k is finite.

The lower limits came from products. Take two colorings with no one-color triangle, on N points with k colors and on M points with ℓ colors. They combine into such a coloring on NM points with k+ℓ colors. Repeating this gives only growth like ck.

OpenAI reports a recursive construction that grows faster. For a constant c>0 and every k≥2:

Rk(3)≥(ck1/3log⁡k)k

So the limit is infinite. With the upper limit, this shows that Rk(3) has the form kΘ(k), which means that log⁡Rk(3) grows like klog⁡k. A later paper extends the method to other odd cycles.
