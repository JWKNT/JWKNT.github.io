  • ℤ is Diophantine in ℚ

    A new manuscript reports one polynomial equation that separates the integers from all other fractions.

  • The Exponential Time Hypothesis

    Two groups report an algorithm that solves 3-SAT faster than every exponential time, but only for enormous inputs.

    Posted 26 October 2026 by two groups. Two separate Lean proofs. Expert review continues.

  • The quasi-Riemann hypothesis

    OpenAI reports that no zero of the Riemann zeta function has real part above 7/8. This limit controls the error in prime counts.

    Posted 6 October 2026. Lean proof of the 7/8 bound. Experts who studied the proof largely agree that it is correct.

  • The Kakeya conjecture in every dimension

    OpenAI reports that a set with a unit segment in every direction always has full dimension, even when its volume is zero.

    Posted 26 October 2026. Lean proof. No published human check found.

  • The Jacobian conjecture is false

    A polynomial map in three variables can preserve volume at every point and still send three points to one.

    Posted 19 July 2026. Anyone can check the example by direct calculation. The two-variable case remains open.

  • Erdős's unit distance problem

    The number of pairs at distance 1 among n points can exceed what Erdős expected. A later report also lowers the upper limit.

    Lower bound, May 2026: checked by outside mathematicians. Upper bound, October 2026: Lean proof.

  • Hilbert's tenth problem over ℚ

    OpenAI reports that no algorithm can decide if every polynomial equation has a solution in fractions.

    Posted 6 October 2026. No Lean proof. Later papers build on it. No published independent check found.

  • The Unique Games Conjecture

    OpenAI reports a proof that some systems of two-variable equations are hard to satisfy even approximately. Many approximation algorithms are then the best possible unless P = NP.

    Posted 6 October 2026. Lean proof. Experts are studying the proof and giving talks on it.

  • Navier–Stokes blow-up

    OpenAI reports that a smooth force on a fluid at rest can produce infinite speed in finite time. The case with no force remains open.

    Posted 8 September 2026. Lean proof. The Clay Mathematics Institute calls the problem apparently settled, and its review continues.

How to read the status lines

Each status line gives the state of public sources on 5 November 2026. A claim of proof can still contain an error. Each page separates these kinds of check.

Preprint
A public manuscript that no journal has reviewed.
Lean proof
A proof in a language that a computer checks step by step. A person must still confirm that the formal statement says what the headline says.
Human review
Study of the proof by experts in the field, reported in public.
Peer review
Formal review for a journal. It usually takes months or years.
