Jacobian Conjecture

A map F from ℂn to ℂn has a matrix of partial derivatives. Its determinant, the Jacobian determinant, is the local scale factor for volume. Where it is not zero, F can be reversed locally.

A local inverse does not give a global inverse. The last map in the table has a nonzero determinant everywhere, yet it repeats its values. The Jacobian conjecture said that polynomials behave better: if F is a polynomial map with a constant nonzero determinant, then F has a polynomial inverse. A polynomial on ℂn with no zeros must be constant, so this condition only says that the determinant is never zero.

The conjecture is false for n≥3. Levent Alpöge posted this counterexample in the variables x,y,z, and credited the AI model Claude Fable 5:

F1=(1+xy)3z+y2(1+xy)(4+3xy)F2=y+3x(1+xy)2z+3xy2(4+3xy)F3=2x−3x2y−x3z

The determinant of F=(F1,F2,F3) is −2 at every point, yet three points have one output:

F(0,0,−14)=(−14,0,0)F(1,−32,132)=(−14,0,0)F(−1,32,132)=(−14,0,0)

Direct calculation confirms both facts. Extra coordinates that the map leaves unchanged give examples for each larger n. The case n=2 remains open.
